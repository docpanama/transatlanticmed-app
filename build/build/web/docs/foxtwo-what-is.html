FoxTwo: A Critical Care Transport Clinical Reasoning Support Tool

By FoxTwo

Executive Summary

FoxTwo is a guideline-informed educational and clinical reasoning support tool designed for critical care transport (CCT) professionals operating in dynamic, resource-constrained, and high-risk environments. It runs as a web app — open the link, no account needed.

It focuses on en route and transport-phase medicine, supporting providers such as flight paramedics, flight nurses, military medical personnel, and advanced prehospital clinicians.

FoxTwo is not a medical decision-making system and does not provide real-time treatment directives. Instead, it reinforces clinical reasoning, highlights transport-specific risks, and aligns discussion with established clinical practice guidelines (CPGs), operational guidelines, and transport medicine principles. Its purpose is to augment provider thinking — not replace clinical judgment, protocols, or medical direction.

Operational Context

Critical care transport exists at the intersection of resuscitation and movement. Providers must manage unstable patients while dealing with:

  • Limited space and access to the patient
  • Equipment constraints and finite oxygen supply
  • Environmental stressors (altitude, vibration, noise, temperature)
  • Dynamic timelines and uncertain evacuation windows
  • Reduced ability to troubleshoot or escalate care mid-transport

FoxTwo is designed specifically for this phase of care — where deterioration can be rapid, interventions are constrained, and anticipation is critical. It supports care across:

  • Rotary-wing and fixed-wing aeromedical transport
  • Ground CCT and interfacility transport
  • Military en route care (MEDEVAC, CASEVAC, AE)
  • Austere and prolonged transport environments

Doctrinal and Clinical Alignment

FoxTwo is structured around established guidance including:

  • Joint Trauma System (JTS) Clinical Practice Guidelines (CPGs)
  • Committee on En Route Combat Casualty Care (CoERCCC) guidelines
  • Critical Care Air Transport Team (CCATT) guidance on ventilation
  • Prolonged Field Care (PFC) clinical practice guidelines
  • Damage Control Resuscitation (DCR) principles
  • Sepsis management in austere environments
  • Analgesia and sedation strategies for prolonged care
  • TBI management in resource-limited settings
  • Interfacility transport operational guidance

It reflects the same “minimum / better / best” and risk-based thinking found in modern military and civilian transport doctrine, adapted to the realities of en route care.

Functional Capabilities in Transport Medicine

1. Clinical Reasoning Support Under Transport Stress

Transport providers operate in cognitively demanding environments with competing priorities. FoxTwo assists by breaking down complex physiology into transport-relevant considerations, highlighting competing priorities (e.g., oxygenation vs hemodynamics vs ICP), reinforcing reassessment frameworks (e.g., MARCH-PAWS in motion), and exploring multiple acceptable management strategies rather than a single answer. Its goal is to strengthen decision-making under pressure — not automate it.

2. Transport-Specific Risk Anticipation

Unlike static clinical settings, transport introduces unique physiologic stressors. FoxTwo emphasizes altitude effects on oxygenation and gas expansion, ventilator limitations and oxygen consumption planning, hemodynamic instability during movement or packaging, risks of sedation, paralysis, and airway loss in confined environments, and equipment failure troubleshooting (e.g., DOPE framework). It focuses on what can go wrong in transit and how to think ahead of it.

3. Bridging Guidelines to Real-World Application

Clinical guidelines are often written for hospital settings or ideal conditions. FoxTwo translates these into practical considerations for limited personnel and equipment, trade-offs between ideal care and feasible care, adaptations for prolonged or delayed transport, and contextual application of protocols rather than rigid adherence. It reinforces that guidelines inform care — but do not replace judgment.

4. Education and Mentorship for CCT Providers

FoxTwo functions like an experienced preceptor by explaining the “why” behind interventions, comparing different approaches and their risks/benefits, highlighting common pitfalls in transport medicine, and supporting scenario-based learning and case discussion. It is particularly useful for new flight clinicians building transport-specific thinking, experienced providers refining advanced decision-making, and training and simulation environments.

Role in the Care Continuum

FoxTwo operates primarily in the en route phase, bridging point-of-injury / prehospital resuscitation, prolonged field care (when applicable), interfacility critical care transport, and transition to definitive care. It emphasizes continuity, reassessment, and communication across these transitions — key elements identified in en route care doctrine.

Limitations

FoxTwo:

  • Does not provide patient-specific medical advice
  • Does not replace protocols, medical direction, or teleconsultation
  • Does not make clinical decisions

All discussions are educational and must be applied within the framework of local protocols, provider scope, and medical oversight.

Bottom Line

FoxTwo is a transport-focused clinical reasoning assistant designed to help critical care transport providers think clearly in complex, high-risk environments. It reinforces structured thinking, anticipates transport-specific complications, and bridges guideline-based medicine with the realities of delivering care in motion. Its value lies not in giving answers — but in helping providers ask better questions, recognize risk earlier, and adapt effectively when conditions are less than ideal.
