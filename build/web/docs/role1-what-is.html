Role1: A Prolonged Care Clinical Reasoning and Decision-Support Assistant

By Role1

Executive Summary

Role1 is a guideline-informed educational and clinical reasoning support tool designed for medical providers operating in prolonged care and austere environments where evacuation is delayed and resources are limited. It runs as a web app — open the link, no account needed.

It supports providers such as:

  • military medics (68W and equivalents)
  • physician assistants
  • flight medics in pre-transport phases
  • humanitarian and remote medicine clinicians

Role1 is not a medical decision-making system and does not provide real-time treatment directives. Instead, it reinforces structured clinical reasoning, supports trend recognition over extended time horizons, and aligns discussion with established clinical practice guidelines (CPGs) and prolonged care doctrine.

Its purpose is to augment provider thinking, not replace clinical judgment, protocols, or teleconsultation.

Operational Context

Prolonged care exists in the gap between initial stabilization and definitive care. Providers may be required to manage critically ill or injured patients for hours to days under conditions that include:

  • limited equipment and medications
  • minimal staffing
  • environmental stressors
  • degraded monitoring capability
  • delayed or uncertain evacuation timelines

These conditions occur across:

  • military Role 1 and forward environments
  • humanitarian and disaster response settings
  • remote industrial and expeditionary operations
  • rural or resource-limited healthcare systems

Role1 is designed for this phase of care, where time, trend recognition, and resource discipline are the dominant factors in patient survival.

Doctrinal and Clinical Alignment

Role1 is structured around established guidance including:

  • Joint Trauma System (JTS) Clinical Practice Guidelines (CPGs)
  • Prolonged Casualty Care (PCC) guidance
  • Tactical Combat Casualty Care (TCCC) principles
  • Damage Control Resuscitation (DCR)
  • Sepsis management in austere environments
  • Analgesia and sedation strategies for prolonged care
  • Documentation and teleconsultation workflows

It reflects the doctrinal progression from immediate resuscitation → sustained stabilization → evacuation planning. Role1 does not generate new doctrine. It reinforces structured, evidence-informed practice under constraint.

Functional Capabilities in Prolonged Care

1. Clinical Reasoning Over Time

Prolonged care is defined by trajectory rather than isolated data points. Role1 assists by reinforcing serial reassessment, emphasizing trend interpretation (vital signs, urine output, mental status), identifying early signs of deterioration, and supporting prioritization across competing physiologic threats. Its focus is decision-making across extended timelines, not just initial intervention.

2. Resource-Constrained Decision-Making

In austere environments, ideal care is often not possible. Role1 helps providers adapt guideline-based care to available resources, prioritize limited supplies (fluids, antibiotics, oxygen), make tradeoffs between competing needs, and plan for resource depletion over time. It emphasizes feasible care over ideal care.

3. Teleconsultation and Escalation Support

Prolonged care often relies on remote consultation. Role1 supports this by structuring consult requests (MIST, SOAP, trend summaries), identifying missing or critical data, organizing clinical information for transmission, and reinforcing escalation thresholds. It supports but does not replace live teleconsultation or medical direction.

4. Documentation and Continuity of Care

Extended care requires disciplined documentation to support shift transitions, handoffs, evacuation, and after-action review. Role1 assists with structured note organization, trend-focused summaries, evacuation packaging, and continuity of care communication.

5. Cognitive Support Under Stress

Providers in prolonged care environments face fatigue, cognitive overload, uncertainty, and competing priorities. Role1 functions as a structured cognitive aid by prompting key reassessment elements, reinforcing decision frameworks, highlighting common failure points, and supporting reflective clinical thinking.

Role in the Care Continuum

Role1 operates primarily in the pre-transport and prolonged stabilization phase, bridging point-of-injury or initial presentation, early resuscitation, extended field or facility care, and transition to transport or evacuation. It emphasizes continuity, reassessment, and preparation for movement, which are key determinants of survival in delayed evacuation environments.

Limitations

Role1:

  • does not provide patient-specific medical advice
  • does not replace clinical judgment, protocols, or medical direction
  • does not perform physical examination
  • depends entirely on user-provided information
  • does not assume operational authority

All outputs are educational and must be applied within provider training, scope of practice, local protocols, and available supervision.

Bottom Line

Role1 is a prolonged care clinical reasoning assistant designed to help providers manage patients in environments where time, constraint, and uncertainty dominate care. It reinforces structured thinking, supports sustained stabilization, and bridges guideline-based medicine with the realities of austere and delayed evacuation settings. Its value lies not in giving answers, but in helping providers recognize deterioration earlier, manage resources more effectively, maintain discipline over time, and prepare patients for safe transition to definitive care.
